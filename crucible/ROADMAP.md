# Performance roadmap

What has been tried, what it was worth, and what is left. Numbers are `-O3`, same machine, both
compilers, each against its own control unless it says otherwise, and every comparison checks that
the binaries produce identical output. The reasoning behind each entry is in `docs/issues/`.

Ahead on twenty of the twenty-one, behind on one.
Over the fourteen Renaissance benchmarks the geometric mean of the ratios is 0.831, that is 17% ahead, with a spread from 39% ahead to 3% behind; the two sessions it is pooled from give 0.832 and 0.829.

The Renaissance rows are from 2026-11-02 and 2026-11-03, the tree of 2026-11-02 with its defaults: a machine with six cores, each binary given eight processors, `-O3`, two sessions of three rounds, the median of the six, Oracle's binary in the same runs. On 2026-09-30, with the copying old generation as the default (item V), cold code compiled for size (item K), and the method-handle adapters (item Y), three rounds on one machine with six processors, the geometric mean over twelve was 0.943, ahead on six, level on two, behind on scrabble, fj-kmeans, rx-scrabble and scala-stm-bench7; the paragraphs that follow are from that day.
The binaries run with the heap each collector chooses. With the compacting old generation the geometric mean was 0.937 to 0.943 as well, but in much more memory on three of them: akka-uct four times Oracle's, scrabble three and a half, scala-stm-bench7 one and a half.
With the heap of each capped at the memory Oracle's binary uses on it, the geometric mean is 0.980 copying and 1.015 compacting, and 0.963 copying with scala-stm-bench7 given the 448 MB where it reaches Oracle's memory without falling off a cliff.
Measured again on 2026-10-01 on the tree as it is, scala-stm-bench7 at 448 MB, two rounds: 0.958, and 0.945 with the sampled recipe of item S; still behind at that memory are scala-kmeans by 5 to 6%, its heap kept well under the cap (item Z), scala-stm-bench7 by 6 to 8%, fj-kmeans by 2 to 3%, and rx-scrabble by 1.6% with the recipe and 7% without.
Over a whole Renaissance run CrucibleVM's binary takes longer than its iterations say: on scrabble the twenty iterations add up to 5.1 s against Oracle's 5.3, and the run to 8.4 s against 6.7. The difference is outside the iterations, much of it the complete collection the harness asks for before each one, 99 ms in ours against 69 in Oracle's.
Until that day the geometric mean was 0.998, with mnemonics, scrabble, and future-genetic behind by 5 to 13%.
The two services are from 2026-09-29, the same machine: each built by its framework's own native build (for Quarkus its argument file, for PetClinic the command Spring's Maven plugin runs), recorded under the load it is timed with, the service on four processors and the load (oha, 16 connections, 20 s of warm-up and 30 s measured a page) on four others, three rounds, and a second profile-guided build of each compiler's image. Without a profile Oracle's compiler is ahead by 11% on Quarkus and by 12 to 22% on PetClinic; CrucibleVM's profile is worth 65 to 82% there, Oracle's 35 to 53%. PetClinic's owner pages answer 500 in Spring's own native image of it as well, so its two other pages are the ones timed.
The rows of the five samples are from 2026-11-03, the machine of the Renaissance rows: Oracle's images as built on 2026-09-29, this tree's built on 2026-11-02 with its defaults, eleven runs each, interleaved, and every image printing what Oracle's control prints.
Oracle's image depends on how long a run it was recorded on, in both directions: BenchPGO runs 786 ms from a recording of 3 million iterations and 372 from one of 300 million, ArrayBench 372 from a short recording and 1188 from a long one.
Oracle's rows are the better of the two. On 2026-09-29 CrucibleVM's images ran within 1% of each other from either: BenchPGO 295 and 294, BranchBench 462 and 461, ArrayBench 321 and 321, JsonBench 1408 and 1422, GameOfLife 2918 and 2903.
Until that day these rows were from an older machine, and BenchPGO was 7% behind there.
Earlier versions of this table had the Renaissance rows from an older and slower machine, and akka-uct and par-mnemonics from one with four processors, so the times here are not comparable with those.

Across Renaissance the profile-guided gain is as large as Oracle's or larger on most benchmarks.
What separates the binaries is what is underneath: where the two controls are level CrucibleVM is ahead, and where Oracle's starts ahead it stays behind by about that much.
See `docs/issues/2026-09-20-renaissance-across-the-suite.md`.
