# GraalVM 25.4

`2026-10-03`

CrucibleVM is based on the tag `vm-25.3.4.1`.
The 25.3 release branch has no commits beyond it, and the 25.4 line, tag `vm-25.4.4.1.1` from 2026-09-17, is 390 commits ahead with the JDK `ce-25.0.4.1.1+1-jvmci-25.4-b23`.
The branch `crucible/upgrade-25.4` merges that tag.

## The Merge

Nine files changed on both sides.
Four of them conflicted:

- _common.json_, _JVMCIVersionCheck.java_, and _ci_includes/publish-javadoc.jsonnet_ take 25.4's version.
- _HostedConfiguration.java_ takes 25.4's version: 25.4 makes the object layout with identity hash codes added on demand the default itself, so this tree's own default for `-H:+OptionalIdentityHashCodes` is removed from _SubstrateOptions.java_.

The tree then builds and passes `mx checkstyle`.

## What Broke

`mx helloworld` builds with assertions on, and the image failed in `ForeignCallNode.setBci` while lowering an exact array copy.
The exact array copy snippets of `-H:InlineExactArraycopy` called the stub for each element kind through a node intrinsic of `ForeignCallNode`.
An intrinsic takes the bytecode index of the enclosing non-intrinsic method when the snippet is parsed, here −6, and instantiating the snippet then sets the index of the copy it replaces, which the assertion refuses.
The same code is in the 25.3 tree; builds without assertions overwrite the index and run correctly.

The stubs are now called through `SubstrateExactArrayCopyCallNode`, in the shape of the generic copy call: it picks the stub for its element kind when it is lowered, after frame states are assigned, and takes the index of the copy once.
Its calls appear only after analysis, so the nine stubs are analysis roots, like the generic copy next to them in `NativeImageGenerator`.

## What It Is Worth

Fourteen Renaissance benchmarks, five rounds each, all binaries timed in the same session at `-O3`: the 25.3 binary from its recording, a new recording with the 25.4 instrumented binary and the binary built from it, and Oracle's profile-guided binary.

| | against Oracle's binary |
| --- | --- |
| 25.3 | 0.944 |
| 25.4 | 0.945 |
| 25.4 with `-H:LargeArrayThreshold=520000` | 0.929 |

25.4 is level with 25.3 over the fourteen.
fj-kmeans, philosophers, scala-kmeans, future-genetic, reactors, and finagle-http are 2 to 5% faster; scala-doku, mnemonics, and rx-scrabble are 2 to 4% slower.
The large-array threshold costs scala-kmeans nothing on 25.4, where it cost 1.7% on 25.3.

A 25.4 binary built from the 25.3 recording separates the recording from the compiler:

| median, ms | Oracle | 25.3 | 25.4 | 25.4 from the 25.3 recording |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| scala-doku | 1240 | 1057 | 1076 | 1078 |
| mnemonics | 1832 | 1603 | 1668 | 1781 |

scala-doku's 2% is 25.4's own.
On mnemonics the old recording is 11% slower on 25.4 and the new one 4%: the lead there rests on `wordCode` being inlined into its caller, and 25.4 tips that decision the other way more often.

akka-uct runs at one of two speeds, about 10.7 s and 12.5 to 13 s an iteration, and in the five rounds of the comparison 25.4 had happened never to run fast.
Over eight more rounds, 25.3 and 25.4 each ran fast in four, with means of 11,576 and 11,773 ms against Oracle's 13,907.

## Control Flow Duplication

25.4 turns on control flow duplication in the community compiler (`-H:+OptDuplication`).
With a profile, BranchBench ran 21 times slower than on 25.3, 9.96 s against 461 ms, from the 25.3 recording and from its own, and BenchPGO 9.5% slower.
Without a profile, 25.4 builds BranchBench in 621 ms at `-O3`, against 900 ms for 25.3.

The profile makes BranchBench's rarely called helper hot enough to be inlined and its loop of 24 iterations fully unrolled, which leaves one large expression without side effects.
Duplicating the merges after the branches gives that expression more than one use, and the schedule computes it where all its uses meet, at the head of the loop.
The disassembly shows 352 instructions run on every iteration before the first branch, against a loop of 29 without duplication.
Turning off the other two phases 25.4 adds, `OptPullThroughPhi` and `OptimizeDiv`, changes nothing; they make BranchBench faster.

A profile now turns duplication off unless it is set explicitly, as `-Os` does.
BranchBench takes 461 ms and BenchPGO 292 to 298 ms again.
Over the fourteen Renaissance benchmarks, built from the same 25.4 recordings, duplication off is 0.8% faster, 0.942 of Oracle's binary against 0.950:

| | duplication off against on |
| --- | --- |
| faster | reactors 7.1%, scala-kmeans 1.9%, rx-scrabble 1.5%, akka-uct |
| level | scrabble, mnemonics, par-mnemonics, future-genetic, finagle-chirper |
| slower | fj-kmeans 0.8%, scala-doku 0.9%, finagle-http 0.9%, scala-stm-bench7 1.5%, philosophers 2.4% |

Duplication off is not free: JsonBench takes 1,474 ms without it and 1,361 ms with it, 8.3% more, in 21 rounds of one session.
No option of the duplication phase keeps that and loses BranchBench's slowdown.
With duplication on, BranchBench takes 9.92 s and JsonBench 1,360 to 1,418 ms with any of these:

- the simulation that schedules floating nodes too (`-H:+ScheduledDuplicationSimulation`);
- the budgets of ordinary code for hot code (`DuplicationBudgetFactorHotCode=0.25`, `DuplicationCostReductionFactorHotCode=64`, `MaxSimulationIterationsHotCode=2`);
- a minimum branch frequency of 0.9 or 0.99 (`DuplicationMinBranchFrequency`);
- unlikely branches kept in the simulation (`-H:-SimulationPruneUnlikelyBranches`);
- no late duplication (`DuplicationBudgetFactorLate=0`).

The value is computed once and used in every copy of the code duplicated after the branches, and placed where the copies meet; keeping both results would take duplicating the value with the code, in the phase itself.

The value is there because the helper's loop was fully unrolled: with duplication on and `-H:-FullUnroll`, BranchBench takes 537 ms and JsonBench keeps its 1,368.
A rule against fully unrolling a loop entered on few of its enclosing loop's iterations would cover BranchBench, but not BenchPGO, which stays at 323 ms with duplication on whether loops are unrolled or not: its loop is a type switch, `Inc` on 31 iterations in 32, and what duplication does to it is a second, separate cost.

## The Configurations on 25.4

With the duplication fix, from new 25.4 recordings, five rounds each in one session on 2026-10-04, against Oracle's profile-guided binary:

| | fourteen | twelve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| counted profile | 0.949 | 0.948 |
| with `-H:LargeArrayThreshold=520000` | 0.933 | 0.929 |
| sampled recipe | 0.927 | 0.926 |
| sampled recipe with the threshold | **0.918** | **0.915** |

With the recipe and the threshold, scala-stm-bench7 (1.068) and rx-scrabble (1.019) are the two still behind.
The recipe is faster than the counted profile on eleven of the fourteen, par-mnemonics by 5.4%, rx-scrabble 3.7%, and scala-doku 3.4%, and slower on future-genetic by 2.8%.
The threshold costs no benchmark: reactors, 8.9% slower in one session of five rounds, is 2.4% faster over ten more, and finagle-chirper is level, 0.7% over ten.

The recipe's cost on future-genetic was a call in a copy the stacks said too little about.
With stacks, the call tree is built from them alone; a call in a copy with fewer than `CrucibleMinimumSamplesAtCall` samples under it got the receivers counted over every path, and the lambda `Mutator.mutate` maps over stayed an indirect call from `ReferencePipeline$3$1.accept`, 763 of 5,099 samples in its own frame, where the counted profile inlines it.
`-H:+CrucibleSampledCountedFallback` keeps the tree built from the counters beside the sampled one and answers such a call from the counted receivers along the same path, 224 to 431 calls a build.
Over the fourteen, two sessions of five rounds, the recipe with the threshold goes from 0.919 of Oracle's binary to 0.909 with it (0.921 to 0.901 in the first session, 0.918 to 0.913 in the second): future-genetic 6.5% faster, scrabble and philosophers 2%, rx-scrabble 1.8% slower, and the rest level, with reactors and akka-uct too noisy to tell.

Startup is level with Oracle's binary on both; PetClinic's peak memory is 166 MB against 394.
